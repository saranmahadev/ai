Neural Networks

Neural Networks1 min read

Not every neural network learns by backpropagation, and not every useful idea about neurons came from deep learning. This planet covers the other lineage: networks that settle into stored memories instead of computing a forward pass, learning rules inspired directly by real synapses, maps that organise themselves with no labels at all, neurons that communicate in discrete spikes, and a handful of architectures training moved past. Several of these ideas turn out to echo straight back into the deep learning you already know. It needs Deep Learning (the perceptron, backpropagation, autoencoders, attention) and Math (vectors, probability); where a topic here builds directly on one of those, it says so under You need.

Each topic asks one question, explains it in plain language with a small worked example, lets you experiment in a bench, and ends with where the idea shows up in practice.

The road

  1. Biological and Historical Foundations: the real neuron, Hebb's rule, and the first mathematical neuron — the roots this whole planet grows from.
  2. Energy-Based Networks: networks that settle toward stored patterns instead of computing a forward pass.
  3. Layer-Wise and Unsupervised Pretraining: the training trick that reopened deep learning in 2006, before end-to-end backpropagation of very deep nets was practical.
  4. Self-Organization and Competitive Learning: maps and codebooks that organise themselves from unlabelled data.
  5. Spiking and Neuromorphic Computing: neurons that communicate in discrete spikes, and the hardware built to run them efficiently.
  6. Alternative Architectures: networks trained by a single solved step or a fixed random reservoir, instead of gradient descent throughout.
  7. Neural Networks in Practice: where these classical ideas resurface in modern deep learning, when to reach for them today, and a capstone that ties the planet together.

From here the road leads on to Transformers, Generative AI, LLMs, RAG and Agents.
