Training Deep Networks

Deep Learning1 min read

Gradient descent and backpropagation (see Gradient Descent and Backpropagation by Hand on the Math planet) are the engine; this district covers the practical decisions that decide whether that engine actually trains a deep network — or stalls, explodes, or wanders for days.

Topics on this stretch of the road:

  • Weight Initialization: starting the weights so signal neither vanishes nor explodes before training begins.
  • Vanishing and Exploding Gradients: why gradients shrink or blow up in deep networks, and what that does to training.
  • Gradient Clipping: capping a gradient's size so one bad batch cannot wreck the weights.
  • Learning Rate Schedules and Warmup: changing the step size over training instead of fixing it.
  • Batch Size and Training Dynamics: how many examples per step changes the noise, speed and endpoint of training.
  • Second-Order and Natural Gradient Ideas: what using curvature, not just slope, buys you, and why it is rarely used directly.

Gradient Descent

Gradient descent finds a minimum by repeatedly stepping downhill: compute the gradient (the uphill direction), move a little the opposite way, and repeat.

Backpropagation by Hand

Backpropagation computes how the loss changes with every weight in a network by applying the chain rule backwards from the output. Its results are exactly the gradients that gradient descent needs.
