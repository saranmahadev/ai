Reasoning

LLMs1 min read

An aligned, instruction-following model still has to decide how to actually arrive at an answer — straight to a conclusion, or by working through intermediate steps the way a person solving the same problem would. This section covers the techniques that get more reliable answers out of a model by changing how it reasons, not what it knows.

Topics in this section:

  • Chain-of-Thought Prompting: asking a model to show its intermediate steps, not just its final answer.
  • Process Reward vs. Outcome Reward: rewarding a correct chain of reasoning versus rewarding only a correct final answer.
  • Tree-of-Thought and Search-Based Reasoning: exploring several reasoning paths instead of committing to the first one.
  • Self-Refinement and Iterative Critique: a model checking and revising its own answer before committing to it.
  • Distilling Reasoning into Smaller Models: training a small model to reason well by learning from a larger one's reasoning traces.
  • Tool-Augmented Reasoning (ReAct-Style): interleaving reasoning steps with calls to an external tool.
  • Test-Time Compute Scaling: spending more computation at answer time, instead of only at training time, to get a better answer.

Tool-Augmented Reasoning (ReAct-Style)

No amount of chain-of-thought or tree search fixes a problem the model simply can't solve on its own — exact large-number arithmetic, a live lookup, running actual code.
