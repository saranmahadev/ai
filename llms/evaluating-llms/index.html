Evaluating LLMs

LLMs1 min read

Knowing whether an LLM is actually good — not just at one cherry-picked example, but systematically — means building and trusting the right measurement tools. This district covers how the field evaluates these models, and the specific ways those measurements can mislead if taken at face value.

Topics on this stretch of the road:

  • Benchmark Suites (MMLU, HELM, BIG-bench): the standardized tests the field uses to compare models.
  • Benchmark Contamination and Leakage: when a model has already seen the test, invalidating the score.
  • LLM-as-Judge Evaluation: using one model to grade another's output, and where that breaks down.
  • Capability Evaluation vs. Safety Evaluation: two different questions — can it, and should it — that need different tests.
  • Leaderboards and Their Limits: what a ranked list of model scores does and doesn't tell you.
  • Adversarial and Jailbreak Evaluation: testing a model against inputs specifically designed to break its guardrails.

Benchmark Suites (MMLU, HELM, BIG-bench)

Comparing two LLMs fairly requires testing them on the same questions, under the same conditions, at a scale no individual could check by hand.

Benchmark Contamination and Leakage

A benchmark score only means something if the model is actually being tested, rather than recalling something it already saw.

LLM-as-Judge Evaluation

Benchmark suites work well for questions with a single checkable right answer. Most interesting LLM output — a summary, a piece of writing, a conversational response — doesn't have one.

Capability Evaluation vs. Safety Evaluation

"Is this model good?" is really two separate questions wearing one trench coat: can it do the task, and will it behave safely while trying?

Leaderboards and Their Limits

A leaderboard promises a simple answer to "which model is best?" — rank them by score, read off the winner.

Adversarial and Jailbreak Evaluation

safety evaluation on ordinary, well-behaved inputs only tells you how a model behaves when nobody's trying to break it.
