Calculus

Math1 min read

Calculus is the maths of change. It answers "how fast is this quantity changing right now?" (differentiation) and "how much has it accumulated?" (integration). For AI, the first question is the important one: training a model means nudging its numbers in the direction that reduces its error, and calculus tells you which direction that is.

Topics in this section:

  • Rate of Change: average and instantaneous change.
  • Limits: what a value approaches.
  • Continuity: functions with no jumps or holes.
  • Derivatives: the slope of a curve at a point.
  • Derivative Rules: shortcuts for common functions.
  • Chain Rule: derivatives of chained functions.
  • Derivatives of Exp, Log and Sigmoid: the functions AI uses most.
  • Higher Derivatives and Curvature: the rate of change of the slope.
  • Partial Derivatives: change in one direction at a time.
  • Gradients: the direction of steepest climb.
  • Jacobian: derivatives of functions with many outputs.
  • Hessian: curvature in many dimensions.
  • Integrals: accumulation and area.
  • Taylor Approximation: simple functions that mimic complicated ones.
  • Automatic Differentiation: how software computes derivatives exactly.

Rate of Change

A rate of change says how much one quantity changes per unit of another, such as kilometres per hour or dollars per item.

Limits

A limit is the value a function approaches as its input gets closer and closer to some point, even if the function never actually reaches (or is not even defined at) that point.

Continuity

A function is continuous at a point when you can draw it through that point without lifting your pen: the limit exists and equals the function's value.

Derivatives

The derivative tells you how fast a function's output is changing at one particular input: the slope of the curve at a point.

Derivative Rules

You do not compute a derivative from the limit every time.

Chain Rule

The chain rule finds the derivative of a function built from other functions: d/dx f(g(x)) = f′(g(x)) · g′(x). Rates multiply along the chain, like gears turning one another.
