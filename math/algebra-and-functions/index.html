Algebra and Functions

Math1 min read

Algebra is arithmetic with unknowns, and a function is a rule that turns inputs into outputs. Together they are the grammar of every model: a model is just a function with adjustable numbers, written in algebra. This section builds from variables and equations through sequences and sums to the specific functions that machine learning uses.

Topics in this section:

  • Variables and Expressions: letters standing for numbers, and formulas built from them.
  • Equations and Solving: finding the number that makes a statement true.
  • Inequalities: describing a whole range of answers.
  • Sequences and Series: ordered lists of numbers and their totals.
  • Sums and Products: the Σ and Π shorthand for long calculations.
  • Functions: rules from inputs to outputs, and how to read them.
  • Graphs and Transformations: drawing a function and shifting, stretching and flipping it.
  • Linear Functions: straight lines, slope and intercept.
  • Polynomials and Quadratics: curves built from powers, and their roots.
  • Exponential and Logarithmic Functions: growth, decay and their inverses.
  • Composition and Inverses: chaining functions and undoing them.
  • Functions AI Loves: sigmoid, tanh, ReLU and softplus.

Variables and Expressions

A variable is a letter that stands for a number that can change or that we do not know yet, and an expression combines variables, numbers and operations into a calculation.

Equations and Solving

An equation says two expressions are equal, and solving it means finding the value of the unknown that makes the statement true.

Inequalities

An inequality compares two expressions with <, >, ≤ or ≥, and its answer is usually a whole range of numbers rather than one.

Sequences and Series

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers that follows a rule, and a series is what you get by adding its terms.

Sums and Products

Summation (Σ) and product (Π) notation write a long repeated addition or multiplication in one short line. They are literally a loop: "for each i from a to b, add (or multiply) this in".

Functions

A function is a rule that takes an input and gives back exactly one output.
