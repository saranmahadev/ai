Arithmetic and Number Sense

Math1 min read

Before vectors and gradients come the numbers themselves. This section makes sure the everyday machinery is solid: what kinds of numbers exist, how fractions and percentages relate, in what order to calculate, what powers and logarithms do, how to handle huge and tiny scales, how rounding behaves, and how to tell when an answer is plausible.

Topics in this section:

  • Numbers and Their Kinds: naturals, integers, rationals and reals, and why the difference matters.
  • Fractions, Ratios and Percentages: three ways to say the same thing.
  • Order of Operations and Properties: which step comes first, and which rearrangements are safe.
  • Exponents and Roots: repeated multiplication and its undoing.
  • Scientific Notation and Scale: writing very large and very small numbers.
  • Logarithms: counting multiplications instead of size.
  • Rounding, Absolute Value and Modulo: floors, ceilings, distance from zero and wrap-around.
  • Estimation and Sanity Checks: getting the right ballpark before the exact answer.
  • Reading Math Notation: the symbols and Greek letters you will meet from here on.

Numbers and Their Kinds

Numbers come in nested families: natural numbers for counting, integers that add negatives, rational numbers that are fractions, and real numbers that fill the whole number line.

Fractions, Ratios and Percentages

A fraction, a decimal and a percentage are three ways of writing the same quantity, and a ratio compares two quantities.

Order of Operations and Properties

An expression such as 2 + 3 × 4 has one right answer because everyone agrees on the order of operations: parentheses first, then powers, then multiplication and division, then addition and…

Exponents and Roots

An exponent says how many times to multiply a number by itself, and a root undoes it.

Scientific Notation and Scale

Scientific notation writes a number as a digit-and-decimal part times a power of ten, so 6,000,000 becomes 6 × 10⁶ and 0.00042 becomes 4.2 × 10⁻⁴.

Logarithms

A logarithm answers "what power do I raise this base to, to get that number?" log₂ 8 = 3 because 2³ = 8.
