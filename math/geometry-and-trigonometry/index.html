Geometry and Trigonometry

Math1 min read

Geometry is the maths of position, distance, angle and shape. It matters for AI because data points are positions in space: closeness means similarity, direction means meaning, and much of Linear Algebra is geometry with a longer list of coordinates. Trigonometry adds angles and circles, which return in rotations, waves and the positional patterns of modern models.

Topics on this stretch of the road:

  • Coordinates and Distance: locating points and measuring how far apart they are.
  • Angles and Radians: two ways to measure a turn.
  • Sine, Cosine and the Unit Circle: the functions of an angle.
  • Lines, Planes and Circles: the basic shapes as equations.
  • Areas, Volumes and Scaling: how size grows when shapes scale.
  • Geometry in Many Dimensions: what changes when there are hundreds of coordinates.

Linear Algebra

Linear algebra is the maths of lists of numbers (vectors) and grids of numbers (matrices), and of the simple operations that combine them.

Coordinates and Distance

A coordinate system locates a point with numbers, (x, y) on a plane, and a distance formula measures how far apart two points are.

Angles and Radians

An angle measures a turn. Degrees split a full turn into 360 parts, and radians measure it by the length of the arc it cuts from a circle of radius 1, so a full turn is 2π.

Sine, Cosine and the Unit Circle

Sine and cosine turn an angle into the height and width of a point on a circle of radius 1, the unit circle.

Lines, Planes and Circles

A line, a circle and a plane are the basic shapes, and each has a short equation.

Areas, Volumes and Scaling

Area measures a flat region and volume measures a solid. When you scale a shape by a factor k, lengths grow by k, areas by k² and volumes by k³.
