Optimization

Math1 min read

Optimization is the art of finding the best values: the parameters that make a model's error as small as possible. Training a model is an optimization problem, and everything before this section, functions, derivatives, gradients and vectors, was preparation for it. This section explains the loss you minimise, the landscape you walk over, the step you take, and the tricks that make the walk faster and safer.

Topics in this section:

  • Loss Functions: turning "how wrong" into a single number.
  • Minima, Maxima and Saddle Points: the flat spots of a landscape.
  • Gradient Descent: walking downhill, one step at a time.
  • Learning Rate: how big each step should be.
  • Convex vs Non-Convex: landscapes with one valley and landscapes with many.
  • Stochastic Gradient Descent: stepping with noisy but cheap estimates.
  • Momentum and Adam: remembering the past to move faster.
  • Constraints and Lagrange Multipliers: optimising within limits.
  • Regularization as a Constraint: keeping models simple.

Loss Functions

A loss function turns "how wrong is the model?" into a single number: small when predictions are good, large when they are bad.

Minima, Maxima and Saddle Points

A minimum is a low point of a function, a maximum a high point, and a saddle point is flat but rises in some directions and falls in others.

Gradient Descent

Gradient descent finds a minimum by repeatedly stepping downhill: compute the gradient (the uphill direction), move a little the opposite way, and repeat.

Learning Rate

The learning rate η sets how big each gradient-descent step is. Too small and training crawls; too large and it overshoots, bounces around, or blows up.

Convex vs Non-Convex

A function is convex if it is bowl-shaped: the straight line between any two points on its graph never dips below the graph.

Stochastic Gradient Descent

Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) takes each step using the gradient from a small random mini-batch of examples instead of the whole data set.
