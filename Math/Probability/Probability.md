Probability is the maths of uncertainty. It gives numbers to "how likely?", and rules for combining those numbers. Modern AI is probabilistic at heart: a classifier outputs the *probability* of each label, a language model outputs a probability for every possible next word, and training itself is often framed as making the observed data as probable as possible.

Topics on this stretch of the road:

* [[Events and Sample Spaces]]: the outcomes that can happen and the groups of them we care about.
* [[Probability Rules]]: the few rules every probability obeys.
* [[Conditional Probability]]: how likely something is once you know something else.
* [[Independence]]: when knowing one thing tells you nothing about another.
* [[Bayes Theorem]]: updating beliefs with evidence.
* [[Random Variables]]: turning outcomes into numbers.
* [[Expectation]]: the long-run average.
* [[Variance and Standard Deviation]]: how spread out outcomes are.
* [[Discrete Distributions]]: Bernoulli, binomial and Poisson.
* [[Continuous Distributions]]: densities, the normal curve and areas as probabilities.
* [[Joint and Marginal Distributions]]: several random quantities at once.
* [[Law of Large Numbers]]: why averages settle down.
* [[Central Limit Theorem]]: why bell curves are everywhere.
* [[Markov Chains]]: randomness that remembers only the present.
