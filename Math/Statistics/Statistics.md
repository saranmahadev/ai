Probability reasons from a known chance process to what data it will produce. Statistics runs the other way: it starts from data and reasons back to what produced it. It supplies the tools for summarising a data set, judging how far a sample can be trusted, estimating unknown quantities and deciding whether a pattern is real, and it also names the many ways data can mislead.

* [[Describing Data]]: centre and spread in a few numbers.
* [[Data Distributions and Outliers]]: the shape of data and its odd points.
* [[Sampling and Bias]]: learning about a population from a sample, and how it goes wrong.
* [[Covariance and Correlation]]: measuring how two variables move together.
* [[Correlation vs Causation]]: when two things move together and when one causes the other.
* [[Estimation and Bias]]: guessing an unknown value from data, and how good the guess is.
* [[Maximum Likelihood]]: picking the value that makes the data most probable.
* [[Confidence Intervals]]: a range that expresses the uncertainty of an estimate.
* [[Hypothesis Testing and P-values]]: asking whether an effect could be chance.
* [[Common Test Mistakes]]: multiple tests, p-hacking and other traps.
* [[Bayesian vs Frequentist]]: two philosophies of probability and inference.
* [[Overfitting in Statistics]]: fitting noise instead of pattern.
