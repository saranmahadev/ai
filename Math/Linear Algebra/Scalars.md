A **scalar** is simply a **single numerical value** that represents a quantity with **magnitude but no direction**.

### Examples

* **5** → scalar
* **−3** → scalar
* **2.5** → scalar
* **100 kg** → scalar quantity
* **30°C** → scalar quantity
* **₹500** → scalar quantity

The key idea is:

> **Scalar = “how much”**

Whereas a **vector** tells you both **how much + which direction**.

| Type       | Example       | Contains              |
| ---------- | ------------- | --------------------- |
| **Scalar** | 50 km/h       | Magnitude             |
| **Vector** | 50 km/h north | Magnitude + Direction |

### In equations

Suppose:

$$
x = 5
$$

Here, \(x\) is a **scalar** because it is just one value.

If:

$$
\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix}3\\4\end{bmatrix}
$$

then is a **vector** because it has multiple components and represents direction.

### Why the word "scalar"?

"Scalar" comes from the idea of a **scale**—something that can be represented by a single number.

This becomes especially important in **linear algebra, physics, machine learning, and computer science**, where you'll frequently see:

$$
\text{scalar} \rightarrow \text{vector} \rightarrow \text{matrix} \rightarrow \text{tensor}
$$

For example, in ML:

$$
w = 0.7
$$

is a scalar weight, while

$$
\mathbf{x} = [2,5,1,8]
$$

is a vector of features.
